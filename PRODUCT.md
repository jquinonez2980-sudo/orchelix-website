## Platform

web

## Users

Owner-led businesses and professional-services firms — the operator or owner-principal
who is accountable for revenue and for whether the phone gets answered. They are not
technical buyers. They evaluate in gaps between real work, often on a phone, and they
have usually been sold automation before and been disappointed.

Confirmed verticals: Architecture & Design, Stone & Fabrication, Field Service,
Manufacturing, Healthcare, Legal.

A significant share of their own customers are Spanish-speaking; the operator may be
bilingual, or may be losing calls because nobody in the office is.

## Product Purpose

Orchelix builds and deploys multi-agent systems that run revenue operations — the
recurring, time-sensitive work that gets dropped when a small team is busy: answering
calls, qualifying leads, following up, and closing the books.

Success is the operator seeing work completed that would otherwise have slipped, with a
reviewable record of every action the system took.

## Positioning

**"Built like a consultancy. Priced like software."**

The mechanism a competitor could not truthfully copy: every deployment is led by a named
senior consultant who maps the workflow before anything is automated, and every agent
action is logged to an audit trail with human approve/override/coach controls. Competitors
sell either software without the consultant, or consulting without the product.

Bilingual EN/ES is native to every agent rather than a translation layer — a real
differentiator in the confirmed verticals and geographies.

## Operating Context

Agents operate inside the tools the business already uses — phone, inbox, CRM, ledger.
Work surfaces in an operator console with a single audit trail across agents.

Engagement shape: a senior consultant maps one workflow, the first agent goes live in
**14 days**, every action is logged and reviewable, and further agents are added only
once the first has earned it.

## Product Principles

1. **Demonstrate, don't assert.** A live Esmi recording outperforms any claim. Where proof
   is absent, use honest capability framing rather than manufactured credibility.
2. **Shipped and roadmap must be visibly different.** Esmi is real; the other two agents
   are not yet. The design must encode that difference rather than flatten it.
3. **The audit trail is the trust product.** Operators adopt automation they can inspect,
   override, and explain to their own auditors.
4. **A named human is part of the offer.** The senior consultant is not support — it is
   the differentiator, and should never read as a help desk.
5. **Bilingual must be demonstrated, not claimed.** A site that claims EN/ES while
   serving a partial Spanish experience disproves its own differentiator.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Full EN/ES parity is a product-truth requirement, not a nice-to-have. The current `/es`
  tree is a hand-duplicated partial (3 of ~12 marketing pages, no i18n system), which
  actively contradicts the positioning above.
- Audience skews non-technical and mobile; interfaces must survive being read quickly on a
  phone in a noisy environment.
- Existing baseline to preserve: skip link, `:focus-visible` ring, `prefers-reduced-motion`
  handling.
